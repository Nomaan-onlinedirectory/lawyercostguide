What Drives the Cost in Buffalo

Complexity of your situation determines costEstate planning cost in Buffalo scales with complexity. A basic will, power of attorney, and healthcare directive for a single person with simple assets costs $800–$1,500. A married couple with children, a home, and retirement accounts needs a more complete plan — typically $2,500–$5,000. Business owners and high-net-worth individuals need more.
Will vs. trust — the probate questionA will is less expensive to create but goes through probate — the court process to validate it. In Buffalo, probate can take 6–18 months and typically costs 3–5% of estate value. A revocable living trust avoids probate entirely, making the upfront cost of a trust plan worthwhile for estates over $150,000.
Powers of attorney are often overlookedMany people focus only on what happens after death and neglect incapacity planning. A durable power of attorney (finances) and healthcare directive (medical decisions) are essential documents that address what happens if you become incapacitated — arguably more immediately important than a will for many people.
Beneficiary designations override your willRetirement accounts (401k, IRA), life insurance, and payable-on-death bank accounts pass directly to named beneficiaries — your will has no effect on these assets. Outdated beneficiary designations (ex-spouses, deceased relatives) are one of the most common and costly estate planning errors.
Estate tax thresholdsFederal estate tax in NY applies only above $13.61 million (2024 threshold). Most Buffalo residents will not owe federal estate tax. Some states have lower thresholds — check whether NY has a separate state estate tax. An estate planning attorney in Buffalo can clarify your specific situation.
Money-saving tip for BuffaloReview your estate plan after every major life event — marriage, divorce, birth of a child, significant change in assets, or death of a named beneficiary. An outdated estate plan can be worse than no plan at all. Many Buffalo attorneys offer discounted annual review packages. Always verify contractor licences with your state licensing board.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does estate planning cost in Buffalo?
Estate planning costs in Buffalo range from $450 for a basic will and powers of attorney to $5,300 for comprehensive trust-based estate plans. The right plan for your situation depends on your assets, family structure, and whether avoiding probate is a priority. Always get a flat-fee quote — estate planning is rarely billed hourly.
Do I need a will or a trust in Buffalo?
A will is sufficient for simple situations — but it goes through probate in Buffalo. Probate typically takes 6–18 months and costs 3–5% of estate value. A revocable living trust avoids probate entirely. For estates over $150,000 in Buffalo, the upfront cost of a trust plan is usually recovered in probate savings alone.
What happens if I die without a will in NY?
Without a will in NY, your assets pass under intestacy laws — a fixed formula that may not match your wishes. Your spouse may not receive everything. Minor children may have assets managed by a court-appointed guardian rather than someone you'd choose. Unmarried partners receive nothing. A basic will costs $450–$875 and solves all of these issues.
What is a power of attorney and do I need one in Buffalo?
A durable power of attorney designates someone to manage your finances if you become incapacitated. Without one, your family would need to petition a Buffalo court for guardianship — a process that costs $3,000–$10,000 and takes months. A power of attorney typically costs $300–$600 as part of an estate plan and can save significant expense.
How often should I update my estate plan in Buffalo?
Review your estate plan after every major life event — marriage, divorce, birth of a child, death of a named beneficiary or executor, significant change in assets, or relocation to a different state. An outdated estate plan can be as problematic as no plan. Many Buffalo estate planning attorneys offer annual review packages at reduced cost.
How do I verify an estate planning attorney's licence in NY?
Verify any NY attorney's licence at No-fault divorce available since 2010 — irretrievable breakdown for 6+ months. For complex estates involving trusts or tax planning, look for attorneys with the Certified Trust and Fiduciary Advisor (CTFA) or Accredited Estate Planner (AEP) designations, which indicate specialist estate planning training.
